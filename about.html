About Irvington Model UN

Model United Nations is a simulation of how countries actually negotiate. Each delegate is assigned a country, rarely their own, and represents its real positions in a committee: researching what that government has said and done, speaking in formal debate, forming blocs with delegations that share its interests, and drafting resolutions that can survive a vote.

We meet every Monday at lunch in room 223, and the travel team meets Mondays after school. Meetings cover parliamentary procedure, including motions, points, and speakers’ lists, along with research methods and how to write a position paper. We run practice debates through the year, and delegates travel to TBD conferences per year at other high schools and universities.

The club is open to all four grades. No experience is required, and there is no tryout; most members join knowing nothing about the UN. Our advisor is Mr. Belmonte, dues are TBD, and the club has been running since TBD.

What delegates take away travels well: speaking to a room without hiding behind notes, finding a credible source quickly and using it, holding an argument together while it is being challenged, and knowing how to work a room to find the few people who will work with you. The friendships made across schools tend to outlast the conferences themselves.
